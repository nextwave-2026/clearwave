# Normalised decline semantics

The canonical event's `normalized_decline_reason` is a closed vocabulary owned by W2. The exact values below match `DECLINE_REASONS` in `detector/schema.py`; do not create aliases or a second vocabulary. The provider's `provider_raw_code` is carried through verbatim and unparsed. When a normalised reason is broad or ambiguous, the raw code may be cited as corroboration from the evidence response, but L4 must not invent its own parser or arithmetic from it.

A reason is a clue, not a root cause. Interpret it with cohort comparisons, payment-level and attempt-level conversion, operational metrics, retries, and confounding evidence. `decline_breakdown` reports reason shares over the explicit `failed_attempts` denominator and compares them with a baseline where available.

## Read the categories before reading the individual codes

Issuer-side refusals, technical failures, and customer-side conditions imply very different causes. They must never be pooled into one undifferentiated "decline rate" when reasoning about cause:

- **Issuer-side or issuer-network decisions:** `do_not_honor`, `issuer_decline`, and `issuer_unavailable` can indicate issuer behavior or reachability. A population-level shift needs comparison across providers, methods, and other issuers.
- **Customer or card-state conditions:** `insufficient_funds`, `expired_card`, `invalid_card`, `incorrect_cvc`, `lost_or_stolen_card`, and `restricted_card` usually describe the instrument or customer's authorization context. A cluster can still reveal a method, region, or issuer policy issue, but the code alone does not establish one.
- **Risk and authentication outcomes:** `suspected_fraud`, `authentication_required`, and `authentication_failed` can involve issuer policy, customer interaction, risk configuration, or an application flow. They need a comparison and operational context.
- **Technical and capacity outcomes:** `processing_error`, `provider_error`, `timeout`, and `rate_limited` point toward a processing path, provider, route, application, or infrastructure condition. They do not by themselves identify which layer failed.
- **Capability and integrity conditions:** `currency_not_supported` and `duplicate` often indicate capability, configuration, idempotency, or retry behavior rather than issuer refusal.
- **Unresolved mapping:** `other` carries little causal information until the raw provider code and surrounding cohorts are inspected.

A decline-reason mix is also not the same as conversion. Payment-level conversion counts distinct customer payments, attempt-level conversion counts provider attempts, and the difference can expose fallback or retry behavior. Never use one pooled decline percentage as a substitute for those two measures.

## Code reference

| Normalised code | What it typically indicates | Failure-mode classes it points toward | What it argues against when concentrated and corroborated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `insufficient_funds` | The issuer or account says the available balance or limit cannot cover the authorization. | Issuing-bank over-decline when the shift is issuer-wide; customer/card-state conditions; sometimes a region or method mix change. | A pure provider timeout, infrastructure outage, or latency fault, unless technical evidence independently shows those faults caused misleading mappings. |
| `do_not_honor` | A generic issuer refusal without a more specific reason. | Issuing-bank over-decline; issuer risk or policy behavior; occasionally a network or route issue that changes issuer responses. | A pure local queue or runtime fault when provider and service metrics are healthy and the reason is concentrated by issuer. |
| `expired_card` | The card's expiration is no longer valid. | Customer/card-state conditions; a payment-method or data-quality problem if it suddenly rises for a broad integration cohort. | A provider degradation or capacity fault when the code is stable across unaffected cohorts and no technical signals move. |
| `invalid_card` | Card data or card status is not accepted as valid. | Customer/card-state conditions; payment-method integration or routing/configuration problems if invalid data is introduced broadly. | An issuer-wide over-decline or provider outage when other evidence shows a stable issuer distribution and no technical shift. |
| `incorrect_cvc` | The card verification value does not match. | Customer/card-state conditions; application or payment-method faults if a release or form change causes the rise. | A provider or infrastructure fault when the code is isolated to input validation and operational health stays normal. |
| `lost_or_stolen_card` | The issuer blocks a card reported lost or stolen. | Customer/card-state conditions; issuer risk behavior if the population-level rate changes. | A technical provider or routing fault when the reason is stable in sibling cohorts and there is no technical correlation. |
| `restricted_card` | The card or account is restricted from this transaction, merchant, geography, or use case. | Issuing-bank over-decline; country or region-specific failure; payment-method or capability restrictions; routing/configuration problems. | A global provider or infrastructure fault if restriction codes are geographically or issuer concentrated and technical metrics are normal. |
| `suspected_fraud` | A risk control or issuer decision flags the attempt as suspicious. | Issuing-bank over-decline; application or risk configuration; country, method, or network-specific risk behavior. | A pure timeout, queue, or capacity explanation when the shift is a clean risk-decision change with healthy technical metrics. |
| `issuer_decline` | A general issuer refusal without a more specific normalised reason. | Issuing-bank over-decline; issuer policy or risk behavior; network effects when an issuer/network cohort is concentrated. | A pure provider-side technical failure when independent issuer traffic is healthy and technical reasons do not rise. |
| `issuer_unavailable` | The issuer or issuer-side network did not provide a usable authorization response. | Issuing-bank over-decline in the broad operational sense; card-network-specific failure; provider or routing degradation if the path cannot reach issuers. | A customer/card-state explanation such as expired or insufficient funds when the failures are transient, widespread, and accompanied by latency or timeout evidence. |
| `authentication_required` | The transaction needs an authentication or customer-challenge step before authorization can complete. | Payment-method failure; application or deployment faults in the challenge flow; routing/configuration or regional capability gaps. | An issuer over-decline or provider outage when the challenge requirement is stable and the failure occurs before an authorization attempt. |
| `authentication_failed` | The required authentication or challenge did not complete successfully. | Payment-method failure; application or deployment faults; country or network-specific authentication paths; issuer risk behavior. | A pure provider latency or infrastructure explanation when authentication failures are isolated to the challenge flow and runtime health is normal. |
| `processing_error` | A generic failure occurred while processing the payment, with no narrower normalised classification. | Payment-provider degradation; routing/configuration problems; application or deployment faults; infrastructure or capacity faults. | A pure customer-side condition or issuer policy explanation when the error is broad across issuers and correlated with service errors or latency. |
| `provider_error` | The provider path returned a technical error. | Payment-provider degradation; routing/configuration problems; application or infrastructure faults that prevent provider processing. | A pure issuer over-decline or customer/card-state explanation when the code is provider-scoped and sibling issuers show the same pattern. |
| `timeout` | The attempt exceeded the response or processing time budget. | Payment-provider degradation; infrastructure or capacity faults; routing/configuration problems; application faults; retry amplification as a consequence. | A direct customer-side decline or a stable issuer policy shift when latency, queue, and timeout evidence are absent. |
| `rate_limited` | A provider, network, service, or account limit rejected or throttled the request. | Routing/configuration problems; provider degradation; infrastructure or capacity faults; application retry amplification. | A normal issuer over-decline or customer card condition when the rate limit is concentrated on a service, connection, or traffic burst. |
| `currency_not_supported` | The selected processing path does not support the transaction currency. | Routing/configuration problems; payment-method failure; country or region-specific failure; provider capability gaps. | A transient provider outage, issuer over-decline, or customer card-state cause when the same currency consistently fails on one path. |
| `duplicate` | The provider or application rejected a repeated or already-seen request under idempotency rules. | Retry amplification; application or deployment faults; routing/configuration or idempotency problems. | A fresh issuer decision or broad provider outage when the code is tied to repeated payment or attempt identities. |
| `other` | The native response did not map to a more specific closed-vocabulary reason. | Any class remains possible; provider, routing, application, or integration problems are common candidates. | Nothing material by itself. It argues against no hypothesis only when raw code and comparison evidence add the contradiction. |

## Operational use

1. Start with `decline_breakdown` for the affected cohort and its baseline. Look for a distribution shift, not merely the largest count.
2. Use `cohort_compare` to determine whether the shift follows provider, issuer, method, country, network, or a broader parent. A reason concentrated in a single observed pairing may be structurally confounded.
3. Use `operational_metrics` for latency, `error_rate`, `timeout_rate`, service health, runtime health, and deployment identity. Use `retry_stats` for attempts per payment, retry depth, amplification, queue depth, and delay.
4. Keep a reason as a competing explanation unless a cited observation contradicts it. A missing `provider_error` observation does not rule out a provider issue, and an `issuer_decline` observation does not prove an issuer fault.
