# Hypothesis discriminators

A discriminator is the smallest next observation that can separate two plausible explanations. Each row names one C2 tool call to request in the affected window and cohort. The response, not the domain pack, is the evidence. If the observation is unavailable or the dimensions are structurally inseparable, keep both explanations and say so.

The best discriminator usually compares the same traffic through a different dimension: the same issuer through another provider, the same method in another country, or the same provider for another issuer. `cohort_compare` returns payment-level conversion, attempt-level conversion, and volume for the target, siblings, and parent. It is therefore the default comparison query. `confounding_check` is the guard when the supposed comparison cells do not actually exist.

| Hypothesis pair | Why the evidence is ambiguous | Single discriminating observation | Evidence tool |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payment-provider degradation vs issuing-bank over-decline | One issuer may use only one provider in the observation window, so every failed issuer attempt is also a failed provider attempt. | The same issuer has a normal conversion through another provider, or the same provider has normal conversion for another issuer. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-provider degradation vs payment-method failure | A method may be routed to one provider, making a provider incident and a method incident share the same cohort. | The method is degraded across providers, or the provider is degraded for sibling methods in the same parent cohort. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-provider degradation vs country or region-specific failure | Provider coverage or connectivity can be different in one geography, so a provider-country slice can support either explanation. | The same provider has the same conversion shift in other countries, or the same country is healthy through another provider. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-provider degradation vs card-network-specific failure | A network may be concentrated on one provider, while a provider may have a network-specific connection issue. | Other networks on that provider remain healthy, or the same network is degraded across independent providers. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-provider degradation vs infrastructure or capacity fault | Both can produce timeouts, high tail latency, retries, and queue growth. | Provider-scoped traffic is unhealthy while the shared service and runtime remain healthy, or service/runtime health, latency, and timeouts are degraded across provider siblings. | `operational_metrics` |
| Issuing-bank over-decline vs customer/card-state conditions | A high count of issuer refusals can be a population-level issuer problem or a change in the mix of legitimate card conditions. | The issuer refusal share rises across a stable issuer population and providers, rather than only specific card-state reasons rising in one narrow cohort. | `decline_breakdown` |
| Issuing-bank over-decline vs card-network-specific failure | An issuer portfolio can be concentrated on one network, and issuer and network effects then move together. | The issuer is degraded on other networks, or other issuers on the same network are degraded while the target issuer is not. | `cohort_compare` |
| Issuing-bank over-decline vs payment-method failure | An issuer may reject one method or authentication path while accepting another, making the method look broken. | The issuer is degraded across methods, or the method is degraded for other issuers while the target issuer is normal. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-method failure vs country or region-specific failure | Local capability, regulation, currency, or connectivity can affect a method only in one geography. | The same method fails in other countries, or other methods fail in the same country while the method is healthy elsewhere. | `cohort_compare` |
| Payment-method failure vs authentication-flow failure | Authentication reasons can be a method-wide integration defect or a separate challenge/customer issue. | The method's failures are concentrated in `authentication_required` and `authentication_failed`, with operational evidence on the challenge path rather than general method conversion loss. | `decline_breakdown` |
| Country or region-specific failure vs currency or routing configuration | A regional drop can be caused by local operations or by a currency/path that is invalid only for that geography. | `currency_not_supported` or another capability reason rises only in the region and method/path, while the same country with a supported path remains healthy. | `decline_breakdown` |
| Card-network-specific failure vs payment-provider degradation | A network-provider connection may fail while the provider works for other networks, or a network may fail wherever routed. | The network is degraded through independent providers, or all networks on one provider are degraded together. | `cohort_compare` |
| Routing or configuration problems vs payment-provider degradation | A bad route can send traffic to a healthy provider incorrectly; a real provider fault can look identical at the selected route. | The same provider is healthy for sibling cohorts that use a different selection context, or the provider is degraded across all independent cohorts using it. | `cohort_compare` |
| Routing or configuration problems vs application or deployment faults | A release can alter route selection or credentials, while a stale route can cause failures without an application release. | `operational_metrics` shows a deployment identity change aligned with a broad service or cohort shift, or shows stable deployment and service health with failure limited to the selected path. | `operational_metrics` |
| Retry amplification vs genuine volume surge | Both increase attempt volume, but only one increases work per customer payment. | `attempts_per_payment`, retry depth, and `retry_amplification_factor` rise above baseline while distinct payment volume does not rise proportionally. | `retry_stats` |
| Retry amplification vs infrastructure or capacity fault | Retries can create queue pressure, while capacity pressure can create the failures that trigger retries. | Attempts per payment rise before queue growth for a stable service, or queue and runtime degradation appear without a preceding increase in retry depth. | `retry_stats` |
| Retry amplification vs payment-provider degradation | Provider timeouts often cause retries, so amplification identifies a mechanism but not the initiating component. | Repeated attempts carry the same provider-technical failure and provider siblings remain healthy, or retries are elevated with no provider-scoped failure and the retry policy changed. | `retry_stats` |
| Application or deployment faults vs infrastructure or capacity faults | Both produce service-facing errors, latency, timeouts, and failed payments across provider siblings. | A deployment identity and service error change align while runtime health remains healthy, or runtime health and queue pressure degrade without a deployment-aligned change. | `operational_metrics` |
| Application or deployment faults vs payment-provider degradation | A provider with most traffic can make a provider failure appear application-wide; an application bug can affect every provider. | Conversion and operational errors shift across provider siblings after one deployment, or only one provider cohort shifts while the service and deployment remain stable. | `cohort_compare` |
| Technical failure vs issuer-side refusal | A generic processing or provider error can be mixed with issuer declines in one pooled decline rate. | The failed-attempt distribution shifts toward `timeout`, `provider_error`, or `processing_error` with latency/errors, rather than toward `issuer_decline`, `do_not_honor`, or other issuer reasons with healthy operations. | `decline_breakdown` |
| Provider or route failure vs an issuer/network reachability issue | `issuer_unavailable` can be caused by issuer-side or network-side reachability, while the route to that network can also be unhealthy. | The same issuer/network is unavailable through independent providers, or only one provider path has timeouts and technical errors. | `cohort_compare` |
| Infrastructure or capacity fault vs a genuine payment-mix change | A traffic mix change can alter aggregate conversion and load without any resource failure. | Parent and sibling conversion move with the mix while service/runtime health, latency, error rate, and timeout rate remain at baseline, or those operational measures degrade independently of mix. | `operational_metrics` |
| Recurring failure vs a new incident | Prior matching incidents may indicate a repeatable weakness, but recurrence does not establish that the current cause is the same. | The historical record shows the same cohort and evidence pattern recurring, or the current cohort and operational signature differ from prior incidents. | `incident_history` |

## Using a discriminator safely

- Ask for the narrowest comparison that changes one causal dimension while holding the affected cohort and window fixed.
- Prefer first-party evidence. `external_status` can corroborate provider degradation, but an unavailable or disagreeing external source does not contradict first-party observations.
- If `confounding_check` reports `structurally_inseparable: true`, the missing comparison is a real limitation. Do not convert a correlation into a ruled-out hypothesis.
- The `missing_evidence` field should name the exact observation from this table, its required comparison cohort, and the hypothesis pair it would separate. The `recommended_next_action` should translate that uncertainty into a human action when confidence is low and impact is high - for example, inspect the named provider dashboard or integration path, notify the merchant or payments owner, or consider a human-approved mitigation. Do not make collecting another comparison the only TAM action.
